# English that lands in India

All output is English. The aim is English an urban Indian viewer reads instantly and recognises as theirs.

## Indian English vs American phrasing (swap table)

| Avoid (US-centric) | Prefer for India |
|---|---|
| "401k", "Roth IRA", "credit score hacks" (US context) | "PF", "SIP", "FD", "CIBIL score" |
| "$5 coffee" | "₹300 cold coffee", "₹20 cutting chai" |
| "Venmo me" | "UPI me", "send it on GPay" |
| "fall semester", "college applications" | "board exams", "JEE/NEET prep", "placement season" |
| "Thanksgiving / Black Friday sale" | "Diwali sale", "Big Billion Days" |
| "Y'all", "dude", "no cap", "bussin'" | plain English; light Indian-English is fine ("so much fun only", "do the needful" only as a deliberate joke) |
| "Rent is due on the 1st" | "salary day", "month-end", "EMI date" |
| "Your boss emails you at 6pm" | "Your manager pings you on Teams at 9 pm" |
| "Miles", "Fahrenheit", "pounds" | "km", "°C", "kg" |
| "$1 million" | "₹1 crore", "₹10 lakh" (lakh/crore, Indian digit grouping: 1,00,000) |

## Local references that work (use specifics, not stereotypes)

- Money: ₹ amounts people actually spend (chai, Swiggy order, metro card, rent in Bengaluru vs Pune), UPI, EMIs, salary credit SMS
- Cities and commute: Bengaluru traffic, Mumbai local, Delhi metro, Gurgaon offices, hostel/PG life
- Cricket: IPL nights, India vs Pakistan, the last-over moment
- Exams and education: board results, JEE/NEET/CAT, coaching in Kota, engineering-college culture
- Work: appraisal season, notice period, WFH vs office, startup layoffs, standups, "quick call?"
- Family: parents' money advice, relatives at weddings, the family WhatsApp group
- Brands: name categories, not specific brands, unless the brand approves; avoid implying endorsement

## Relatable Indian situations (hook starters)

- "When the salary hits on the 1st and it's gone by the 5th"
- "Explaining your startup job to your relatives at a wedding"
- "Your Bengaluru rent vs your Bengaluru salary"
- "Board results day in an Indian household"

## Clarity for viewers with mixed English proficiency

- 8–12 words per spoken sentence; on-screen beats of 3–7 words
- Common words over clever ones ("save" not "squirrel away"; "cheap" not "a steal")
- One idea per beat; numbers as digits (₹500, 3 tips)
- No puns or idioms that only work in American English
- Say it and show it: on-screen text repeats the key spoken words

## Tone

- Confident, warm, slightly self-deprecating works well; avoid preachy or "hustle bro"
- Respect regional diversity: don't make one city or state the punchline
